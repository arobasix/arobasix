The Main Obstacles to Bridging Innovation and Trust

A comparison of the obstacles identified by ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini.

Shared obstacles

  • Opacity: users cannot always understand why a system produced a given answer.
  • Convincing errors: AI can be fluent and confident while being wrong.
  • Unclear responsibility: it is often difficult to determine who answers for a harmful outcome.
  • Conflicting incentives: speed and commercial pressure can compete with testing, safety and privacy.
  • Concentration of power: a small number of organisations control models, data, infrastructure and computing capacity.
  • Bias and inequality: systems may reproduce or amplify social discrimination.
  • Security and misuse: the same technology can support a useful application or a sophisticated attack.
  • Unequal access: prosperity cannot be shared if only a few countries or groups can benefit.
  • Democratic fatigue: deepfakes and contradictory information can weaken trust in institutions.

Trust that can be verified

Bridging innovation and trust requires replacing blind trust with verifiable trust. Five questions should have clear answers:

  1. Who designed the system?
  2. What data was used to train it?
  3. In which situations can it fail?
  4. Who controls its uses?
  5. Who is responsible for the consequences?

Trust should not mean that AI never makes mistakes. It should mean that its limits are known, its errors can be detected, its decisions can be challenged and someone assumes responsibility.
